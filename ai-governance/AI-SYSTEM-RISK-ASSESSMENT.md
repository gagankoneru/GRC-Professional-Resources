[AI governance](README.md)

## 2. Impact and applicability review

Document foreseeable impacts on individuals, groups, organizational objectives, and service continuity. Consider unfair treatment, inaccurate decisions, privacy, transparency, accessibility, dependency, and accountability.

For each concern record the scenario, affected stakeholders, severity, uncertainty, and evidence. Obtain appropriate domain, privacy, legal, and assurance input. Determine applicable obligations in the [obligations register](../frameworks/README.md); do not infer a statutory classification from an internal risk score.

Use the organization's approved [risk criteria](../risk-management/README.md). Record evidence of meaningful human oversight, communication of limitations, complaints/redress arrangements where relevant, and supplier responsibilities. Reference specialist evaluations through their scope, date, conclusions, and unresolved findings; do not reproduce technical test procedures here.

An internal approval cannot waive an external obligation. Unknown material impacts or missing evidence should be visible in the decision, not hidden in an aggregate score.

## 5. Lifecycle oversight

Review on scheduled dates and material changes to purpose, affected population, supplier, data use, autonomy, obligations, or reported harms. Track complaints, incidents, overdue actions, and oversight effectiveness. Record who can restrict or discontinue use and how retirement obligations are closed.

Public context: the [NIST AI RMF](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) provides voluntary AI risk guidance. This template is independent and does not claim complete alignment or certification.
