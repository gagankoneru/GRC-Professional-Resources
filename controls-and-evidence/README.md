# Control Ownership and Evidence Management

[Resource library](../README.md)

Maintain stable internal control IDs and map obligations separately. A control owner is accountable for the outcome; an operator performs the activity; an evidence custodian maintains the record; an assessor evaluates it.

Describe the activity sufficiently for assurance review without including configuration instructions. Record ownership changes and maintain coverage when staff or suppliers change.

## Acceptance checks

- Relevance: the record supports the exact control and assessment objective.
- Completeness: scope, population, period, omissions, and sampling limits are clear.
- Authenticity: source and custody are traceable.
- Timeliness: the evidence covers the required period and frequency.
- Results: failures and follow-up actions remain visible.
- Protection: access, retention, and disposal follow approved information-handling rules.

Do not equate document existence with effective operation. Preserve failed and superseded records as required by the retention policy. An evidence gap remains a gap until resolved or explicitly reported as a limitation.

Keep operational evidence in approved private storage. Public templates should contain fictional placeholders only. See [assurance guidance](../assurance/README.md) for evaluating evidence and findings.
